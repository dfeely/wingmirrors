Wing mirror covers and caps: choosing and replacing

A scuffed or broken cap does not always mean a whole new mirror. Here is how to tell.

What is a wing mirror cover?

A wing mirror cover, also called a cap, is the outer plastic shell on the back of the mirror. It is the part you see from the side. It is usually body coloured, black or a contrasting colour, and it is the part that gets scraped in car parks.

Cap or full unit?

  • Cap only: the cap is scuffed, cracked or has fallen off, and the glass, motor and indicator work.
  • Full unit: the housing underneath is broken, the motor has failed or the indicator wiring is damaged.

Before you order a cap

  1. Confirm the side. See wing side mirror.
  2. Confirm the generation and year of your car.
  3. Note whether your mirror has an indicator, puddle light or camera that sits in or near the cap.
  4. Find your paint code if you want a colour match.

Fitting a cap

Many caps clip on and can be replaced with a trim tool in a few minutes. Our guide on how to replace a wing mirror cap covers the steps. Methods differ between makes, so check your handbook.

Frequently asked questions

Can I replace just the cover on my wing mirror?

Often yes, if the rest of the mirror is undamaged. Check that a cap is available for your model, side and year.

Do I have to paint a new cap?

Many caps come in primer and need painting. Some are supplied in black or in a colour. Check what you are buying.

Where do I find my car's paint code?

It is usually on a plate in the engine bay, door jamb or in the service book. Your handbook should say where.
