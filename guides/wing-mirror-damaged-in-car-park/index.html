Wing mirror damaged in a car park: what to do

Coming back to a smashed or hanging mirror is annoying. Here is a sensible order of actions.

1. Record what you find

  • Take clear photos of the damage and the surrounding area, including the car's position.
  • Note the time, date and place.
  • Look for witnesses, nearby CCTV or a dashcam.

2. Check whether anyone left details

Some people leave a note. If the car park belongs to a shop, centre or council, ask whether they have CCTV and how to request footage.

3. If you damaged someone else's mirror

If you clip another vehicle, you are generally expected to leave your details or report it. Check current rules and guidance for your situation, as requirements can apply.

4. Decide whether to claim

A mirror repair is often cheaper than a typical insurance excess, and a claim can affect your premium. Compare the repair cost with your excess before deciding. Ask your insurer about your policy, especially if another driver is identified.

5. Get it repaired

  • Cracked glass only: replace the glass.
  • Broken housing or electrics: replace the whole unit.
  • Scuffed cap: replace the cap.

Frequently asked questions

Should I claim on my insurance for a smashed wing mirror?

It depends on your excess and the repair cost. Mirrors are often cheaper to fix than the excess, so compare both and ask your insurer.

Can I drive with a damaged wing mirror?

Only if it gives you a clear, secure view. A mirror hanging by wires or with badly broken glass is unsafe and should be fixed first.
