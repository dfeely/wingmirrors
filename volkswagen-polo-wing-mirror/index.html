Volkswagen Polo wing mirror: generations, types and replacement

Which Volkswagen Polo mirror you need, how generations differ and what to check before you order.

Volkswagen Polo generations and mirror differences

GenerationYearsMirror notes
9N / 9N32001-2009Older cars with simple mirrors. Many are manual or basic electric.
6R / 6C2009-2017The most common Polo today. A 2014 facelift sits within this range, so mirror details can vary.
AW2017 onwardsNewer generation with a different shape. Higher trims add more options.

Year ranges are approximate and based on European sales. Check your registration and handbook for your car.

Which Volkswagen Polo mirror do you have?

Features depend on year, market and trim. Cars like yours may come with:

  • Manual adjustment on base trims of older cars
  • Electric adjustment on many trims
  • Heated glass on some trims
  • Indicator repeater on later cars
  • Folding on some higher trims

Test your own car: does the mirror adjust electrically, fold, show an indicator, or clear mist with the rear screen heater on? Match those features when ordering.

Common Volkswagen Polo wing mirror problems

  • Cracked glass from parking knocks
  • Broken or lost caps
  • Loose glass on older cars

Tip for your Polo

Many Polo repairs are glass-only. If the housing looks fine and the mirror still adjusts, start there.

Glass, cap or full unit?

If only the glass is cracked, glass alone is usually enough. A broken housing, a dead motor or a failed indicator usually means a full unit. See glass vs full unit and replacing a wing mirror.

Volkswagen Polo wing mirror FAQs

Is a Polo mirror cheaper than a Golf mirror?

Often it is a simpler part, but cost depends on the features. A heated, folding mirror costs more than a basic manual one.

How can I tell what generation my Polo is?

Check the registration year. As a rule, the generation changes around 2009 and 2017, but check your handbook for your car.

Which side is the driver's side Volkswagen Polo wing mirror in Ireland?

The right-hand (offside) mirror. The left-hand (nearside) mirror is the passenger side.
